    The 60-second tour

    1. Chat (c): ask Claude anything about the bot, have it act for you (each action waits for your Approve), or paste a contract address for an instant metrics card plus Claude's read. It runs on your Claude plan through Claude Code, not an API key.
    2. Live: your money and positions. Every token is clickable and opens its detail panel: gate checklist, the model’s P(2x), why it says so, holders, and the live tape.
    3. Launch radar: new pump.fun launches, best first. Score is the rule engine’s rating. P(2x) is the trained model’s chance the token doubles before it falls 30%. The T G W marks show which social links (X, Telegram, website) the launch has.
    4. Analytics: what is and isn’t working, with highlights in plain English, a projection of the next 100 trades, and an audit of every filter.
    5. Controls: the risk dial (also on Live) scales trade size, positions and the daily loss limit in one move; turn strategies on or off and adjust the main limits while it runs. In Chat, “more risk” asks Claude to raise the dial, and you approve it.

    How a trade happens

    1. Gates drop launches with red flags: the dev sold or bought too much, bundled supply, serial deployers, copycat tickers, or an insider wallet cluster found from the funding graph.
    2. Confirmation waits for real demand: enough unique buyers, fees paid and net SOL inflow. The rule score must clear Min entry score. If Min P(2x) is set, the model must clear it too.
    3. Sizing buys $5–$20 depending on signal strength and the model’s quarter-Kelly edge, and never more than the hard cap. Defense mode halves size after a losing run.
    4. Exits: take initials (sell enough to recover the cost) at the first target, then ride with a trailing stop that tightens as the run grows. Leave on momentum decay or a stall, and get out before graduation. Red flags (dev sells, an insider cluster grows) sell everything at once.
    5. Graduation plays (on in paper; check their row in Analytics before going live) buy strong momentum late on the curve and sell before migration. Copy trading mirrors leader wallets. Callouts hold a $1 bag so a coin can be called out on pump.fun.

    What P(2x) means, and when to trust it

    It is a calibrated probability from a model trained on your recorded feed: given everything visible right now (curve, flow, holders, socials, insider share), how often did tokens like this double before falling 30% within 10 minutes? “Calibrated” means 30% should come true about 30% of the time. The calibration chart in Analytics checks exactly that on launches the model never saw.

    • Train it: scripts/start.sh train (uses everything in data/feed-*). Retrain weekly: pump.fun behaviour drifts, and published research finds models don’t carry over well across time.
    • Trust it only when the held-out AUC is above 0.65 on your own data. Below that, leave Min P(2x) at 0 and it is display-only.
    • A model trained on the simulated market is meaningless for real money. It only proves the pipeline works.

    Tune without fooling yourself

    1. Change one thing at a time, in paper mode.
    2. Prove it on data first: scripts/start.sh sweep --grid exit.stop_loss_pct=20,30,40 tunes on older launches and tests on newer ones it never saw. It only recommends a change that wins on the unseen part.
    3. Check the gate audit. A gate whose rejects double more often than your buys is too strict. One whose rejects rarely double is earning its keep.
    4. Watch capture (how much of each run you keep) and the MAE highlight (how far winners dip before running) before moving stops.

    Safety

    • Use a dedicated hot wallet holding only the bot’s budget, never your main wallet. The key stays in a file that only the signing code reads. It is never logged, never typed into chat, and never committed.
    • Live trading needs MEME_TRADER_CONFIRM_LIVE=yes on purpose. Paper is the default.
    • Limits always on: the per-trade dollar hard cap, the daily loss limit, the drawdown kill switch, and defense mode after a losing run.
    • This dashboard only answers on 127.0.0.1. Reach it from another machine through an SSH tunnel, never by opening the port.
    • Most pump.fun tokens go to zero. Only trade money you can afford to lose. Nothing here is financial advice.
